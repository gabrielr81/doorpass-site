How to stop ticket sharing at the door

Nobody can stop a guest from forwarding a QR code. What you can do is make sure only one person gets in on it, and catch the second attempt at the door.

Get started See the live demo

The steps

  • Treat every ticket as single-use. The first scan admits the guest. The ticket is marked used from that moment.
  • Watch for amber. If someone shows a copy or screenshot after the real guest was scanned, the screen turns amber with a Check ID message.
  • Ask for ID. Read the message on screen and ask the person for ID. Staff decide whether to admit; DoorPass shows what happened.
  • Send red screens to the door lead. Red means do not admit. Staff do not argue at the line; they send the guest to the door lead.
  • Use Undo and Scan out correctly. Undo within one minute fixes a wrong scan. For re-entry events, use Scan out when a guest leaves so their return is not flagged.
  • Check the activity afterward. The admin keeps an activity history of the night, so you can review duplicate attempts.

This catches duplicates, not forwarding. If the original guest arrives first, the copy is the one that gets stopped, so keep the door lead close to the line.

Questions venues ask

Can someone screenshot a ticket and share it?

They can copy it, but only the first scan admits. The second scan shows amber with a Check ID message so staff can ask for ID.

What does amber mean?

The ticket was already used. Read the message and ask for ID.

What should staff do on red?

Do not admit. Send the guest to the door lead.

Do guests or staff need to download an app?

No. Guests get their QR ticket by email. Staff open DoorPass in a browser on a phone or laptop and use the camera to scan.
